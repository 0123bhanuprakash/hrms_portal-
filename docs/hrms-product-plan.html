HRMS product plan: from records to a working day

What each module does today, what is missing, what an enterprise customer will expect, and which dashboards, widgets and quick actions would make the product feel alive. Functionality first; visual polish follows once we agree on this.

Summary

The data model is already rich: attendance, leave with project-based approval, timesheets, expenses, assets, help desk, reports, roles and delegation. What makes the reference dashboards feel enterprise is not more modules, it is how the product surfaces that data: every person lands on a page built for their role, sees what needs them first, and can act without hunting through menus.

The three biggest wins, in order

1. Role-based home dashboards (employee, manager, HR, super admin) with a “Needs your action” list and real widgets. 2. One approvals inbox for leave, attendance, timesheets, expenses and assets, with impact hints such as leave overlaps. 3. Lifecycle workflows — onboarding and exit checklists that pull HR, IT and Finance tasks together. All three reuse data we already store.

Experience-wide improvements

all roles

Missing Find and act fast

  • Global search and command palette (Ctrl K): people, pages, requests, tickets, assets A
  • One approvals inbox across modules, with bulk approve and “approve all that match attendance” A
  • Approve or reject straight from the notification and email B
  • Saved views and filters on every list; column chooser; CSV/Excel export B

Missing Guidance

  • Company setup checklist for new tenants (locations, holidays, leave types, invite people, roles) A
  • Empty states that say what goes here and how to add the first item A
  • Per-employee activity timeline (joined, role changes, leave, assets, tickets) B
  • Inline help and a short “what’s new” panel C

Enterprise expects Reach

  • Installable mobile web app: check-in, apply leave, approve, log hours B
  • Hindi and other languages; date, number and currency formats per company C
  • Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard, focus, contrast B

Dashboard & quick actions Consistency

  • One date-range picker, one KPI tile, one chart style, one status-pill set used everywhere A
  • Every list page opens with 3–4 summary tiles (e.g. Assets: available / with people / warranty ending) A
  • Lazy-loaded dialogs, charts and photos; lighter org chart and Who’s off A

People & core HR

employees, HR, managers

Have today

  • Employee records with departments, designations, levels, locations, reporting lines
  • My profile with encrypted PAN, Aadhaar and bank details; documents with private storage
  • Org chart, people directory, Spotlight (birthdays, anniversaries), CSV import

Missing

  • Onboarding checklist: tasks for HR, IT (laptop, email), Finance (bank, PF), manager (buddy, first-week plan), employee (documents) A
  • Exit checklist: resignation, notice, asset return, access removal, final settlement hand-off, exit interview A
  • Probation and confirmation workflow with manager review A
  • Employee lifecycle history: promotions, transfers, title and manager changes, effective-dated B
  • Profile change requests that HR approves (bank, address, name) B

Enterprise expects

  • Letter templates: offer, appointment, address proof, experience, relieving — generated as PDF with company letterhead B
  • Document expiry tracking (visa, passport, certifications) and acknowledgement of policies B
  • Custom fields per company; legal entities and cost centres C
  • Bulk actions: change manager, department or location for many people B

Dashboard & quick actions

  • Profile completeness meter for employees; “missing bank details” count for HR
  • Onboarding board: people joining, tasks done, blockers
  • Quick actions: add employee, start onboarding, generate letter, start exit

Attendance

employees, managers, HR

Have today

  • Web check-in and check-out with late minutes, policies, holidays and shifts
  • Corrections with project-based approval; team view; export; missing check-out reminder

Missing

  • Grace rules made visible (“2 of 3 grace uses left”) and late-mark to half-day / loss-of-pay rules A
  • Overtime calculation and compensatory off earned from extra hours or weekend work B
  • Shift roster with rotation and weekly-off patterns per team B
  • Monthly attendance lock before payroll, with a change log after the lock B

Enterprise expects

  • Office IP or geo-fence for check-in; selfie check-in on mobile B
  • Biometric device import (CSV / API) C
  • Anomaly alerts: repeated late arrivals, missing punches, long hours B

Dashboard & quick actions

  • Today ring (worked vs shift), this-week bars, on-time rate, average check-in and check-out
  • Manager: 14-day presence trend, late and missing-punch counts
  • Quick actions: check in/out, fix a missed punch, request WFH

Dashboard & quick actions

Suggested order

Phase A

Makes what we have feel enterprise; reuses current data.

  • Four role dashboards with Needs your action
  • One approvals inbox with overlap hints and bulk approve
  • Global search (Ctrl K)
  • Onboarding and exit checklists; probation workflow
  • Leave accruals and year-end run; grace and late rules
  • Notification preferences and digest; two-factor sign-in
  • Super Admin usage, seats, system health and security events
  • Report filters and drill-down; company setup checklist

Phase B

Depth enterprise buyers ask for in demos.

  • Overtime, comp-off, shift roster, attendance lock
  • Letters, lifecycle history, document expiry
  • Help desk knowledge base and business hours
  • Expense advances, mileage, payout batches
  • Asset QR, audits, licences
  • SSO, approval-chain builder, DPDP requests
  • Mobile web app; Finance and PMO dashboards

Phase C

New modules and integrations.

  • Payroll (when you un-park it)
  • Performance, then Recruitment
  • Surveys, custom reports, billing, integrations (Slack, Teams, WhatsApp)

Every item ships with tests under the rule in CONTRIBUTING.md. Visual polish of each page happens as it is touched, following the dashboard concepts.
